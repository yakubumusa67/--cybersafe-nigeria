Common Cyber Threats

⏱ Estimated Time: 20 Minutes

What is a Cyber Threat?

A cyber threat is a potential danger that can damage, steal, expose, or disrupt digital systems, devices, networks, or information.

Cyber threats can target individuals, businesses, governments, schools, banks, and other organizations.

1. Malware

Malware means malicious software. It is designed to damage systems, steal information, spy on users, or perform other harmful activities.

Examples include:

  • Viruses
  • Worms
  • Trojans
  • Spyware
  • Ransomware

2. Phishing

Phishing is a form of social engineering where attackers try to trick people into revealing sensitive information.

A phishing message may pretend to come from a bank, social media platform, company, friend, or another trusted organization.

Warning signs can include:

  • Unexpected messages asking for passwords
  • Suspicious links
  • Urgent requests for money
  • Requests for verification codes
  • Spelling or grammar mistakes

3. Ransomware

Ransomware is malware that can prevent victims from accessing their files or systems. Attackers may demand money in exchange for supposedly restoring access.

Keeping reliable backups and maintaining updated security software can help reduce the impact of ransomware.

4. Password Attacks

Weak or reused passwords can make accounts easier to compromise.

Attackers may attempt to guess passwords or use passwords obtained from previous data breaches.

Protect yourself by:

  • Using long and unique passwords
  • Using a password manager when appropriate
  • Enabling multi-factor authentication
  • Never sharing passwords or verification codes

5. Social Engineering

Social engineering involves manipulating people into performing actions or revealing information that could compromise their security.

The attacker may use trust, fear, urgency, curiosity, or authority to influence the victim.

6. Denial-of-Service Attacks

A Denial-of-Service (DoS) attack attempts to make a service or system unavailable to legitimate users.

A Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS) attack uses many systems or devices to generate the traffic.

🛡️ How to Stay Safe

  • Think before clicking unknown links.
  • Keep your operating system and applications updated.
  • Use strong, unique passwords.
  • Enable multi-factor authentication.
  • Back up important files.
  • Be careful with unexpected messages and attachments.
  • Never share passwords or verification codes.
  • Report suspicious activity.
