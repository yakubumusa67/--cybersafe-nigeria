Social Engineering

⏱ Estimated Time: 20 Minutes

What Is Social Engineering?

Social engineering is the use of deception and psychological manipulation to trick people into revealing information, giving access, sending money, or performing an action that benefits an attacker.

Instead of attacking a computer directly, an attacker may target the person using the computer.

🚨 Common Social Engineering Attacks

  • Phishing: Fake messages designed to steal information.
  • Smishing: Phishing attacks delivered through SMS or messaging apps.
  • Vishing: Fraudulent phone calls designed to manipulate victims.
  • Impersonation: Pretending to be a trusted person or organization.
  • Baiting: Offering something attractive to trick someone into taking an unsafe action.
  • Pretexting: Creating a believable fake story to obtain information.

🎣 Recognizing Phishing

Phishing messages often create urgency or fear. They may tell you that your account will be closed, your payment failed, or that you must act immediately.

Example:

"URGENT! Your bank account will be suspended. Click this link immediately to verify your account."

🚩 The urgency and unexpected link are warning signs.

🧠 Why Social Engineering Works

Attackers often take advantage of normal human emotions and behaviors.

  • Fear
  • Curiosity
  • Trust
  • Urgency
  • Greed
  • Authority

A message may appear to come from a bank, employer, friend, delivery company, government organization, or another trusted source.

🛡️ How to Protect Yourself

  1. Slow down when a message creates unusual urgency.
  2. Verify unexpected requests through an official channel.
  3. Do not click suspicious links or attachments.
  4. Never share passwords or verification codes.
  5. Check the sender carefully.
  6. Use official websites instead of links in suspicious messages.
  7. Report suspicious messages when appropriate.

💰 Social Engineering and Online Fraud

Social engineering is commonly used in online scams. An attacker may pretend to be a friend, business, bank employee, online seller, or other trusted person.

The goal may be to convince the victim to send money, disclose confidential information, or provide access to an account.

If someone pressures you to act immediately, stop and verify the request independently.

✅ Remember

  • People can be targeted just like computers.
  • Never trust a message simply because it looks official.
  • Urgency is a common manipulation technique.
  • Verify important requests independently.
  • Think before you click, reply, pay, or share information.
