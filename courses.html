Course 3: Phishing Awareness

Welcome to Course 3 of the CyberSafe Nigeria Cybersecurity Program. In this lesson, you will learn what phishing is, how cybercriminals use it to steal information, and the steps you can take to protect yourself.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand what phishing is.
  • Identify different types of phishing attacks.
  • Recognize warning signs of phishing.
  • Learn how to respond safely.
  • Protect your personal and financial information.

What is Phishing?

Phishing is a cyber attack where criminals pretend to be trusted people or organizations to trick victims into revealing sensitive information such as passwords, bank details or verification codes.

Information Attackers Try to Steal

  • Passwords
  • Bank account details
  • ATM card information
  • One-Time Passwords (OTP)
  • Email accounts
  • Social media accounts
  • Personal identity information

Types of Phishing

  • Email Phishing
  • SMS Phishing (Smishing)
  • Voice Phishing (Vishing)
  • Social Media Phishing
  • Fake Website Phishing
  • QR Code Phishing

Warning Signs

  • Messages creating urgency.
  • Requests for passwords or OTPs.
  • Suspicious website addresses.
  • Poor grammar and spelling.
  • Unexpected prizes or rewards.
  • Unknown attachments.

Example of a Phishing Message

"Congratulations! You have won ₦500,000. Click this link immediately and enter your bank details to receive your reward."

This is a common scam. Always verify unexpected offers through official channels before responding.

How to Protect Yourself

  • Never share your password.
  • Never share your OTP.
  • Verify links before clicking.
  • Enable Two-Factor Authentication.
  • Keep your device updated.
  • Think before you click.
  • Use trusted websites only.
  • Report suspicious messages.

Real-Life Scenario

You receive an email claiming to be from your bank asking you to "confirm your account immediately" by clicking a link. Instead of clicking, open your bank's official app or website yourself, or contact the bank through its official customer service number to verify the request.

Course Summary

  • Phishing is a common online scam.
  • Attackers often pretend to be trusted organizations.
  • Never share passwords or OTPs.
  • Always verify suspicious messages.
  • Use strong security practices every day.

Course 4: Social Media Security

Welcome to Course 4 of the CyberSafe Nigeria Cybersecurity Learning Program. Social media platforms help us connect with friends, family and businesses, but they can also expose us to scams, account theft and privacy risks. This course will teach you how to use social media safely and responsibly.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand social media security.
  • Identify common social media threats.
  • Protect your online accounts.
  • Manage your privacy settings.
  • Recognize fake accounts and scams.

What is Social Media Security?

Social media security is the practice of protecting your accounts, personal information and online activities from unauthorized access, scams and cyber threats.

Popular Social Media Platforms

  • Facebook
  • Instagram
  • WhatsApp
  • X (Twitter)
  • TikTok
  • Telegram
  • LinkedIn
  • YouTube

Common Social Media Threats

  • Account hacking
  • Fake profiles
  • Phishing messages
  • Identity theft
  • Fake giveaways
  • Investment scams
  • Romance scams
  • Malicious links

How to Protect Your Account

  • Create a strong password.
  • Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA).
  • Never share your password.
  • Never share your OTP.
  • Review your privacy settings regularly.
  • Log out from public devices.
  • Keep your apps updated.
  • Only accept friend requests from people you know.

Privacy Tips

  • Avoid posting your home address.
  • Do not share your bank details online.
  • Think carefully before posting personal photos.
  • Limit who can see your posts.
  • Review app permissions connected to your account.

Fake Account Warning Signs

  • Very few friends or followers.
  • No original photos.
  • Requests for money.
  • Poor grammar and spelling.
  • Promises of easy money.
  • Urgent requests for personal information.

Real-Life Scenario

A stranger sends you a message claiming you have won a smartphone and asks you to pay a "delivery fee." Before sending any money, verify the promotion through the company's official website or customer support. Be cautious of requests that create urgency or ask for payment upfront.

CyberSafe Tips

  • Think before you post.
  • Verify before you trust.
  • Never click suspicious links.
  • Report fake accounts.
  • Use unique passwords.
  • Update your recovery email and phone number.
  • Stay informed about new scams.

Course Summary

  • Protect your accounts with strong passwords.
  • Enable Two-Factor Authentication.
  • Be cautious of fake profiles and scams.
  • Protect your personal information.
  • Practice responsible social media use.

Course 5: Email Security

Welcome to Course 5 of the CyberSafe Nigeria Cybersecurity Learning Program. Email is one of the most widely used communication tools in the world, but it is also one of the most common ways cybercriminals launch attacks. This course will teach you how to identify suspicious emails and keep your inbox secure.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand why email security is important.
  • Identify suspicious emails.
  • Recognize dangerous attachments.
  • Avoid email scams.
  • Protect your email account.

What is Email Security?

Email security is the practice of protecting your email account, messages and personal information from unauthorized access, phishing attacks, spam and malware.

Common Email Threats

  • Phishing emails
  • Spam messages
  • Malicious attachments
  • Fake invoices
  • Business Email Compromise (BEC)
  • Identity theft
  • Malware downloads
  • Account takeover attempts

Warning Signs of Suspicious Emails

  • Unknown sender.
  • Urgent requests for action.
  • Requests for passwords or OTPs.
  • Poor grammar and spelling.
  • Unexpected attachments.
  • Suspicious website links.
  • Promises of prizes or money.

How to Protect Your Email

  • Use a strong password.
  • Enable Two-Factor Authentication.
  • Never share your login details.
  • Check the sender's email address carefully.
  • Scan attachments before opening them.
  • Delete suspicious emails.
  • Keep your email application updated.
  • Sign out when using public computers.

Safe Email Habits

  • Think before clicking links.
  • Verify unexpected requests.
  • Keep your recovery email and phone number updated.
  • Regularly review your account activity.
  • Report phishing emails when possible.

Real-Life Scenario

You receive an email claiming to be from your university asking you to confirm your login details by clicking a link. Before responding, visit your university's official website directly or contact the institution using its published contact details to confirm whether the message is genuine.

CyberSafe Tips

  • Never send passwords through email.
  • Protect your OTP codes.
  • Verify every unexpected attachment.
  • Ignore offers that sound too good to be true.
  • Always think before you click.

Course Summary

  • Email security protects your personal information.
  • Always verify suspicious emails.
  • Never reveal passwords or OTPs by email.
  • Use strong passwords and Two-Factor Authentication.
  • Delete or report suspicious emails.

Course 6: Mobile Device Security

Welcome to Course 6 of the CyberSafe Nigeria Cybersecurity Learning Program. Smartphones and tablets store personal information such as contacts, photos, emails, banking apps and important documents. This course teaches you how to protect your mobile devices from cyber threats.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand mobile device security.
  • Protect smartphones and tablets.
  • Identify common mobile threats.
  • Learn safe app installation practices.
  • Improve mobile privacy.

What is Mobile Device Security?

Mobile device security is the protection of smartphones and tablets against unauthorized access, malware, scams and data theft.

Why Mobile Security Matters

Many people use their phones every day for communication, online banking, shopping, learning and work. A compromised device could expose sensitive information or allow unauthorized access to your accounts.

Common Mobile Threats

  • Malicious Apps
  • Phishing Messages
  • Fake Wi-Fi Networks
  • Spyware
  • Ransomware
  • Screen Lock Bypass Attempts
  • Data Theft
  • SIM Swap Scams

How to Protect Your Phone

  • Use a strong screen lock.
  • Enable biometric authentication if available.
  • Install apps only from trusted sources.
  • Keep your operating system updated.
  • Enable automatic security updates.
  • Lock your phone when not in use.
  • Back up important files regularly.
  • Use Two-Factor Authentication for important accounts.

Safe App Installation

  • Read app reviews.
  • Check the developer's name.
  • Review requested permissions.
  • Avoid installing unknown apps.
  • Remove apps you no longer use.

Public Wi-Fi Safety

Public Wi-Fi networks may not always be secure. Avoid accessing sensitive accounts, such as online banking, over public Wi-Fi unless you trust the network and take appropriate precautions.

Lost or Stolen Phone

  • Lock the device remotely if possible.
  • Change passwords for important accounts.
  • Inform your mobile service provider if necessary.
  • Monitor your accounts for unusual activity.

CyberSafe Mobile Tips

  • Keep Bluetooth off when not needed.
  • Never share your screen lock PIN.
  • Protect your OTP codes.
  • Update apps regularly.
  • Use trusted chargers and accessories.
  • Review app permissions regularly.

Real-Life Scenario

You download a free flashlight app that requests access to your contacts, camera and location. Before granting permissions, consider whether those permissions are necessary for the app's function. If they seem unrelated, it may be safer to choose a different app.

Course Summary

  • Protect your phone with a strong screen lock.
  • Install apps from trusted sources.
  • Keep your device updated.
  • Be cautious when using public Wi-Fi.
  • Back up important information regularly.

Course 7: Digital Privacy & Personal Data Protection

Welcome to Course 7 of the CyberSafe Nigeria Cybersecurity Learning Program. Every day, we share information online while using websites, apps and social media. This course teaches you how to protect your personal data and maintain your digital privacy.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand digital privacy.
  • Identify personal information.
  • Protect sensitive data online.
  • Reduce your digital footprint.
  • Browse the internet safely.

What is Digital Privacy?

Digital privacy is the protection of your personal information while using computers, smartphones and the internet. It allows you to control who can access your personal data.

Examples of Personal Information

  • Full Name
  • Home Address
  • Phone Number
  • Email Address
  • Date of Birth
  • Bank Details
  • National ID Number
  • Passport Information
  • Biometric Information

Why Privacy Matters

If your personal information falls into the wrong hands, it could be used for identity theft, fraud or unauthorized access to your accounts. Protecting your information helps reduce these risks.

Common Privacy Risks

  • Oversharing on social media.
  • Using weak passwords.
  • Connecting to unsafe Wi-Fi.
  • Downloading unknown applications.
  • Sharing personal documents carelessly.
  • Accepting unknown friend requests.

How to Protect Your Privacy

  • Use strong passwords.
  • Enable Two-Factor Authentication.
  • Review privacy settings regularly.
  • Share only necessary information.
  • Keep your software updated.
  • Log out from shared devices.
  • Delete accounts you no longer use.
  • Be careful what you post online.

Understanding Your Digital Footprint

A digital footprint is the record of your online activities, such as posts, comments, photos and website visits. Think carefully before posting because online content may remain available for a long time.

CyberSafe Privacy Tips

  • Think before you post.
  • Limit the amount of personal information you share publicly.
  • Review app permissions regularly.
  • Protect important documents.
  • Verify websites before entering personal information.
  • Use secure internet connections.

Real-Life Scenario

You receive an online form asking for your National ID number, bank details and password to "verify your account." Before providing any information, verify that the request comes from a legitimate source and consider whether all the requested information is necessary.

Course Summary

  • Digital privacy protects personal information.
  • Share only necessary information online.
  • Manage your digital footprint carefully.
  • Review privacy settings regularly.
  • Practice safe online habits every day.
