Course 1: Introduction to Cybersecurity

Welcome to CyberSafe Nigeria's first cybersecurity course. This course is designed for complete beginners who want to understand how cybersecurity protects people, organizations and governments from cyber threats.

What is Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, smartphones, networks, applications and sensitive information from hackers, cybercriminals and malicious software.

Cybersecurity helps to protect:

  • Bank accounts
  • Email accounts
  • Social media accounts
  • Mobile devices
  • Websites
  • Personal information
  • Businesses and organizations
  • Government systems

Why is Cybersecurity Important?

Every second, thousands of cyber attacks happen around the world. Cybercriminals steal passwords, hack social media accounts, spread malware and scam innocent people online.

Learning cybersecurity helps you stay safe while using the internet.

Examples of Cyber Threats

  • Phishing attacks
  • Identity theft
  • Malware attacks
  • Online scams
  • Ransomware
  • Password theft
  • Social engineering attacks
  • Data breaches

The CIA Triad

Cybersecurity is built upon three important principles known as the CIA Triad.

  • Confidentiality - protecting information from unauthorized access.
  • Integrity - ensuring that information remains accurate.
  • Availability - making sure information is accessible when required.

Best Practices

  • Never share your passwords.
  • Always update your devices.
  • Enable Two Factor Authentication.
  • Avoid suspicious links.
  • Use strong passwords.
  • Install trusted applications only.
  • Always logout from public devices.
  • Backup important files regularly.

Real Life Example

Imagine someone receives a message saying:

"Congratulations! Your bank account has been selected for a ₦500,000 reward. Click the link below immediately."

A cybersecurity-aware person will first verify the message before clicking any links. Cybersecurity teaches us how to identify such scams before becoming victims.

Course Summary

In this lesson you have learned:

  • What cybersecurity means.
  • Why cybersecurity is important.
  • Common cyber attacks.
  • The CIA Triad.
  • Cybersecurity best practices.
  • How cybersecurity protects people online.

Mini Quiz

1. What is cybersecurity?

Answer: The practice of protecting devices, networks and information from cyber attacks.

2. What does CIA stand for?

Answer: Confidentiality, Integrity and Availability.

3. Name two cyber threats.

Answer: Phishing attacks and malware attacks.

Course 2: Password Security and Authentication

Welcome to Course 2 of CyberSafe Nigeria's Cybersecurity Learning Program. In this lesson, you will learn how passwords protect your online accounts and why strong authentication practices are important.

What is a Password?

A password is a secret combination of characters used to protect your accounts, devices and sensitive information from unauthorized access.

Why are Strong Passwords Important?

Weak passwords can easily be guessed or stolen. Cybercriminals often use automated tools that can try thousands of password combinations in a short period of time.

Examples of Weak Passwords

  • 12345678
  • password
  • abcdef
  • qwerty123
  • yourdateofbirth
  • yourfirstname123

Examples of Strong Passwords

  • Musa@CyberSafe2026!
  • Protect#Nigeria2030
  • Learn$CyberSafe24!
  • Digital@Security55!

A Strong Password Must Have:

  • Uppercase Letters
  • Lowercase Letters
  • Numbers
  • Special Characters
  • 12 or More Characters

Never Use:

  • Your Name
  • Your Phone Number
  • Your Date of Birth
  • Your Bank PIN
  • Your Favourite Team's Name
  • Easy Password Patterns

Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

Two-Factor Authentication provides an additional layer of security by requiring a second form of verification before granting access to an account.

Examples of 2FA

  • SMS Verification Codes
  • Authentication Applications
  • Email Verification Codes
  • Biometric Authentication

Protect Your OTP

Your One-Time Password (OTP) is private information. Never share it with anyone. Legitimate organizations will not ask you to disclose your OTP unnecessarily.

Password Managers

Password managers securely store and generate strong passwords, making it easier to avoid reusing passwords across different services.

CyberSafe Tips

  • Use different passwords for different accounts.
  • Enable Two-Factor Authentication whenever possible.
  • Never save sensitive passwords on public devices.
  • Regularly review your account security settings.
  • Avoid sharing passwords with other people.
  • Keep your recovery information up to date.
  • Lock your devices when they are not in use.

Real Life Scenario

You receive a message saying:

"Your account will be suspended within 30 minutes. Kindly send us your password and OTP immediately."

This is a major warning sign. Never provide your password or OTP in response to unexpected requests. Verify such messages through official channels before taking any action.

Course Summary

  • Passwords protect your information.
  • Strong passwords improve account security.
  • Never reuse passwords across multiple accounts.
  • Enable Two-Factor Authentication.
  • Protect your OTP at all times.
  • Use password managers responsibly.
  • Practice good password hygiene.

Mini Quiz

1. Should you share your OTP with anyone?

Answer: No.

2. What does 2FA stand for?

Answer: Two-Factor Authentication.

3. Name two characteristics of a strong password.

Answer: It contains numbers, symbols, uppercase and lowercase letters and is at least 12 characters long.

Course 3: Phishing Awareness

Welcome to Course 3 of CyberSafe Nigeria's Cybersecurity Learning Program. In this lesson, you will learn how cybercriminals use phishing attacks to deceive people into revealing sensitive information and how to recognize common warning signs.

What is Phishing?

Phishing is a cybercrime technique where attackers impersonate trusted individuals or organizations in an attempt to steal sensitive information such as passwords, verification codes, or financial details.

Examples of Information Attackers Target

  • Passwords
  • Email Addresses
  • One-Time Passwords (OTP)
  • Banking Information
  • Personal Information
  • Social Media Accounts
  • Business Credentials

Common Types of Phishing

  • Email Phishing
  • SMS Phishing (Smishing)
  • Voice Call Scams (Vishing)
  • Social Media Scams
  • Fake Websites
  • QR Code Scams

Warning Signs of Phishing

  • Messages that create urgency.
  • Suspicious website links.
  • Unexpected prize announcements.
  • Poor spelling and grammar.
  • Requests for passwords or OTP codes.
  • Threats that your account will be suspended immediately.
  • Requests for sensitive information.

Example One

"Congratulations! You have won a brand new smartphone. Click the link below within five minutes to claim your reward."

Always verify such claims independently before clicking any links or providing personal information.

Example Two

"Your bank account has been blocked. Kindly provide your PIN and OTP immediately."

Legitimate organizations generally provide official channels for account assistance and should not unexpectedly request sensitive information in this manner.

How to Stay Safe

  • Never share your passwords.
  • Protect your OTP codes.
  • Verify website addresses carefully.
  • Enable Two-Factor Authentication.
  • Avoid clicking suspicious links.
  • Keep your devices updated.
  • Use trusted applications only.
  • Verify unexpected requests independently.

Social Media Phishing

Cybercriminals may impersonate friends, businesses or public figures to request money or personal information. Be cautious of unexpected messages asking for urgent assistance or sensitive details.

Email Security Tips

  • Check the sender's email address carefully.
  • Avoid downloading suspicious attachments.
  • Verify unexpected requests.
  • Look for unusual language or formatting.
  • Report suspicious emails when appropriate.

CyberSafe Challenge

Today's challenge is simple. Review one recent email or text message and ask yourself:

  • Do I recognize the sender?
  • Is the message creating unnecessary urgency?
  • Is anyone requesting sensitive information?
  • Does the website address look legitimate?

Course Summary

  • Phishing attacks attempt to steal information.
  • Always verify unexpected requests.
  • Protect your passwords and OTP codes.
  • Avoid suspicious links and attachments.
  • Practice safe browsing habits.
  • Think before you click.

Mini Quiz

1. Should you share your OTP with someone claiming to be customer support?

Answer: No.

2. Name two examples of phishing attacks.

Answer: Fake emails and fake websites.

3. What should you do before clicking an unfamiliar link?

Answer: Verify that it is legitimate.

Course 4: Social Media Security

Welcome to Course 4 of CyberSafe Nigeria's Cybersecurity Learning Program. Social media platforms connect billions of people worldwide, but they can also be targeted by scammers and cybercriminals. This lesson teaches you how to use social media safely and responsibly.

What is Social Media Security?

Social media security involves protecting your accounts, personal information and online activities from unauthorized access, scams and other cyber threats.

Popular Social Media Platforms

  • WhatsApp
  • Facebook
  • Instagram
  • X (formerly Twitter)
  • TikTok
  • Telegram
  • LinkedIn
  • YouTube

Common Social Media Threats

  • Account Hijacking
  • Phishing Attacks
  • Fake Giveaways
  • Impersonation Accounts
  • Fake Investment Opportunities
  • Malicious Links
  • Identity Theft
  • Social Engineering Scams

How Criminals Target Social Media Users

Cybercriminals may attempt to trick users into revealing passwords, sending money or sharing sensitive information. They often pretend to be trusted individuals or organizations.

Examples of Social Media Scams

  • Congratulations! You've won a prize.
  • Your account will be suspended immediately.
  • Click here to receive free airtime.
  • Send ₦5,000 and receive ₦50,000 instantly.
  • Urgent! Your friend needs financial assistance.

Protecting Your Accounts

  • Enable Two-Factor Authentication.
  • Use strong and unique passwords.
  • Never share your OTP codes.
  • Regularly update your passwords.
  • Review your privacy settings.
  • Avoid suspicious links.
  • Log out from shared devices.

Privacy Settings Matter

Avoid publicly sharing sensitive information such as your home address, banking details, identification documents or other personal information that could increase privacy risks.

Social Engineering Awareness

Social engineering attacks manipulate people into revealing information or performing actions that compromise security. Be cautious of unexpected requests that create pressure or urgency.

CyberSafe Security Checklist

  • Do I use Two-Factor Authentication?
  • Is my password strong and unique?
  • Have I reviewed my privacy settings?
  • Do I verify suspicious messages?
  • Do I avoid unknown links?
  • Do I regularly update my applications?

Real Life Scenario

You receive a message from an account claiming to belong to a celebrity offering an investment opportunity that promises unrealistic returns if you send money immediately.

Before taking any action, verify the authenticity of the account and carefully evaluate the claim. Offers that pressure you to act immediately deserve additional scrutiny.

Best Practices

  • Think before you post.
  • Verify information before sharing it.
  • Use secure passwords.
  • Enable Two-Factor Authentication.
  • Be cautious of fake accounts.
  • Protect your personal information.
  • Practice respectful and responsible online behaviour.

Course Summary

  • Social media accounts should be protected carefully.
  • Never share your passwords or OTP codes.
  • Review privacy settings regularly.
  • Be cautious of suspicious messages and links.
  • Practice safe and responsible social media habits.

Mini Quiz

1. Should you share your OTP code online?

Answer: No.

2. Name two social media threats.

Answer: Account hijacking and phishing attacks.

3. What should you do before trusting an unexpected message?

Answer: Verify that it is legitimate.
